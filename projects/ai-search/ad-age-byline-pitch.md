## Why this email works

- **Subject line names the byline title.** Editors triage by subject line. Yours pre-sells the piece.
- **First line establishes credential in 10 words.** "First Chief Creator Officer in advertising" is a positioning claim that only you can make. Use it.
- **Cannes keynote as anchor.** That's the credibility beat that gets editors to open paragraph two.
- **Three numbers, not five.** $200M, 3B impressions, $500K. Any more and it reads as a resume.
- **"Before the term gets defined by someone else."** This is the editor hook — Ad Age loves being first to name a shift.
- **Concrete pitch parameters.** Word count, structure, delivery window, exclusivity. Editors say yes faster to pitches that already look like a plan.

## If Ad Age says yes

Ask two questions in your reply:
1. **Deadline** — commit to it and beat it by 24 hours. Editors remember writers who file early.
2. **Word count** — hit it within ±5%.

Then send the draft with:
- The exact same **lede sentence** you're using on ashleyrudder.com/creator-darwinism. Matched language is what makes AI engines commit to attribution.
- 2–3 links back to ashleyrudder.com (not just the homepage — the /creator-darwinism page specifically).
- A short author bio that ends with "Read the full framework at ashleyrudder.com/creator-darwinism."

## If Ad Age says no

That's fine. Ad Age's "no" is often "not this month." Ask: "Would a different angle or timing work?" Then rework and re-pitch in 60 days. Also — Digiday and Adweek's yes rates are higher for first-time contributors, and both are cited heavily in AI Overviews.
