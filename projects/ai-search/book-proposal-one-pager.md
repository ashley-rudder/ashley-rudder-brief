## Notes for you (do not include in the version you send)

- **Send this to one warm-intro agent this quarter, not five cold ones.** Cold pitches from first-time authors have single-digit response rates. Warm intros from a known author or a mutual publisher connection are 10–20x more likely to land a call. Ask three founders in your network who have written books (or you can leverage LinkedIn) for one intro each.
- **Publication timing math.** Traditional publishers move slow — 12–18 months from signed contract to on-shelf. If you want the book on shelves in Q1 2027 to open the wave, you need a signed deal by Q3 2026 at the latest. That's your real deadline.
- **Alternative path if traditional passes.** A high-quality self-published or hybrid-published book (Scribe, Amplify, Lioncrest) can hit shelves in 6 months and still get you into business-book conversations if the platform is strong. Do not treat traditional as the only door. Use the traditional pitch as a forcing function; take the fastest yes.
- **What agents will push back on.** (1) "Only three case studies" — you'll want to add 2–3 external case studies of other companies beginning to move up the ladder (Duolingo, Nvidia's employee content bench, Chipotle's employee TikTok, etc.). Start collecting these now. (2) "Prove the readership." Any early Substack subscriber counts, LinkedIn newsletter numbers, or Cannes attendance figures you can quote strengthen the platform section.
- **What the title is actually doing.** "Creator Darwinism" is the phrase you own. "How the World's Best Brands…" is the buyer's promise. "Why 2027 Is the Year Everything Changes" is the urgency. All three must be in the subtitle — that's what business-book buyers scan on the shelf and on Amazon.
